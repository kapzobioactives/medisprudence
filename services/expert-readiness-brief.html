Expert Readiness Brief

Right specialty, right documentation, Daubert readiness — before you commit the expert budget. Prevents the most expensive mistake in medical-legal litigation: retaining the wrong expert.

Founder's Advantage

Before committing expert budget, you need to know what the defense will argue medically — not just what the treating physician believes clinically. Dr. Kasturi's payer reviewer background means he can identify which medical theory the defense is likely to advance, which documentation gaps will be targeted in cross-examination, and whether the record currently supports the causation theory the expert will need to defend. This is not generic expert-preparation advice. It is a targeted assessment of whether the record is ready for expert engagement — calibrated to actual defense review methodology.

What the brief covers

  • 01
    Specialty Match

    The correct expert specialty for the specific medical issues — orthopedic surgeon vs. neurosurgeon, neurologist vs. physiatrist — based on what the record actually requires, not what the attorney initially assumed.

  • 02
    Key Record Issues

    The specific medical issues the expert will need to address — and what the record currently supports vs. what needs to be strengthened before the expert reviews it.

  • 03
    Daubert Preparedness Assessment

    Whether the causation theory has adequate record support to survive a Daubert challenge. Identifies the documentation that needs to be in place before expert engagement.

  • 04
    Documentation Action List

    Specific documentation that the treating physician can provide or strengthen before the expert reviews the file — preventing the expert from identifying the same gaps the defense will exploit.

The cost of skipping this

You retain an orthopedic surgeon for a case that actually requires a neurosurgeon. The expert's opinion is immediately limited in scope by their specialty — and the defense knows it.

You retain an expert before the record is ready. The expert identifies the same documentation gaps the defense will attack — and now the expert has seen them on the record.

Your causation theory requires imaging documentation that was never cross-referenced by the treating physician. The expert's opinion is unsupported on Daubert challenge.
